NSTA submits recommendations for the Bandon Road Corridor Upgrade

The North Sydney Transport Association has lodged a submission supporting the proposed Bandon Road Corridor Upgrade while calling for future rail duplication provisions and improved bus interchange at Vineyard Station.

The North Sydney Transport Association has lodged a submission regarding the proposed Bandon Road Corridor Upgrade in Sydney's North West Growth Area.

While supporting the broader project and acknowledging its benefits for road safety, congestion reduction and access, the Association has raised two key concerns regarding infrastructure around Vineyard Station.

Future-proofing the Richmond Line

NSTA has requested that the rail bridge or underpass structure carrying the Richmond Line over Bandon Road be designed to allow for a future second rail track.

While duplication of this section of the Richmond Line is not currently proposed, Vineyard and surrounding areas are expected to experience significant growth. Providing space for a future second track now would minimise future cost and disruption if increased rail capacity is later required.

This could be achieved through sufficient bridge width or bridge abutments capable of supporting future duplication works.

Improving bus–rail interchange

The Association is also concerned that the proposed relocation of the Bandon Road and Wallace Road intersection further from Vineyard Station may worsen bus–rail interchange.

Routes 671 and 679 currently use this intersection area for stops that partly serve passengers accessing Vineyard Station. If these stops are moved further away as a result of the new intersection layout, passengers may face a longer and less accessible walk between bus and rail services.

Vineyard Station as a local transport hub

Vineyard Station is currently the second least-used station on the Sydney Trains network. NSTA believes that direct feeder bus infrastructure would represent a relatively low-cost opportunity to increase patronage, broaden the station's catchment area and support Vineyard's transition into a more significant local transport hub as surrounding development proceeds.

To address this, the Association recommends dedicated bus stop infrastructure within the immediate Vineyard Station precinct.

This could allow Routes 671 and 679 to travel via Bandon Road, turn around at Vineyard Station, and then continue to Wallace Road, or operate in the reverse direction. This would reduce walking distances, improve accessibility for passengers with mobility impairments, and better support Vineyard Station as a local transport interchange.

Such infrastructure could also be used by Route N71 and other current or future bus services that would otherwise stop some distance from the station.

However, the Association wishes to raise two concerns regarding infrastructure around Vineyard Station.

Firstly, the Association requests that the rail bridge/underpass structure carrying the Richmond Line over Bandon Road be designed to allow for a future second rail track. While duplication of this section of the Richmond Line is not currently proposed, Vineyard and surrounding areas are expected to experience significant growth. Providing space for a future second track now would minimise future cost and disruption if increased rail capacity is later required. This could be achieved through sufficient bridge width or bridge abutments capable of supporting future duplication works.

Routes 671 and 679 currently use this intersection area for stops that partly serve passengers accessing Vineyard Station. If these stops are moved further away as a result of the new intersection layout, passengers may face a longer and less accessible walk between bus and rail services.

Vineyard Station is currently the second least-used station on the Sydney Trains network. The provision of direct feeder bus infrastructure would represent a relatively low-cost opportunity to increase patronage, broaden the station's catchment area, and support Vineyard's transition from one of Sydney's least-used stations into a more significant local transport hub as surrounding development proceeds.

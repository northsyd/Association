Media Release / 10 August 2026

NSTA secures two major transport advocacy outcomes at North Sydney Council.

The Association will contribute to the Neutral Bay–Mosman project stakeholder process, while renewed heritage bus shelters will incorporate transport and timetable information.

Media Release

10 August 2026

North Sydney Transport Association

The North Sydney Transport Association (NSTA) secured two significant outcomes at North Sydney Council on 10 August 2026, following advocacy on the Neutral Bay to Mosman Route project and the renewal of North Sydney's heritage bus shelters.

A role in the Neutral Bay–Mosman project

Council approved the revised concept design for the Neutral Bay to Mosman Route: Street, Walking and Cycling Upgrades, following extensive public discussion.

NSTA supported the project while calling for young transport users to have a formal role in its detailed design.

“Young people shouldn't simply be the users of the transport network being designed around us. We should have a voice in designing it.”

During debate, Councillor Beregi moved to add the North Sydney Transport Association and Neutral Bay Synagogue to the stakeholder list alongside bus service operators.

The amendment was carried, followed by the substantive motion. NSTA will now contribute to the project's detailed design process as an advisory stakeholder.

“We aren't asking to replace technical expertise or community consultation. We're asking to contribute our perspective as young people who experience the transport network differently.”

Bus shelter renewal to include passenger information

Council also unanimously approved the Heritage Bus Shelters Renewal Works under Tender 6/2026, following NSTA advocacy for improved passenger information.

“They are heritage assets, but they are also pieces of public transport infrastructure used by people every day.”

Council subsequently amended the recommendation to require that transport and timetabling information be incorporated within the bus shelters.

The amendment reflects NSTA's call for clear route, destination, stop and timetable information to be provided alongside the shelters' heritage features.

“We simply encourage Council to ensure that, while preserving the heritage character of these shelters, it also preserves their most important function: providing a safe, useful and understandable place for people to catch a bus.”

NSTA also raised the future of the remaining critically deteriorated shelters. Of the 22 shelters identified as being in critical condition, 12 are currently prioritised for renewal.

The Mayor assured Council that the remaining shelters “will not be left behind.”

A significant night for youth transport advocacy

NSTA President Lachlan Steenson said the two outcomes represented practical examples of young people contributing to local transport decisions.

“We came to Council tonight with two very practical tasks: that young people have a seat at the table for the cycleway's detailed design, and that renewed heritage bus shelters remain genuinely useful to passengers. We're incredibly pleased to see both reflected in the Council's decisions.”

NSTA will continue engaging with Council and other stakeholders as both projects progress.

About NSTA

The North Sydney Transport Association (NSTA) is NSW's largest active youth-led transport advocacy organisation focused on improving public transport, active transport and transport accessibility through research, community engagement and constructive advocacy.

Out Without a Bang: The Missed Farewell for Sydney's K Sets

After more than four decades of service, Sydney's K Sets quietly left the network without a public announcement, farewell service or opportunity for passengers to say goodbye.

15 June 2026 • North Sydney Transport Association

Type
Commentary

Date
15 June 2026

Topic
K Set retirement

Final service
Run 38-E
Redfern to Leppington

Back to news

At 6:42pm on Monday 15 June 2026, an 8-car K Set arrived at Leppington on run 38-E, having departed Redfern at 4:55pm via the City Circle.

With that arrival, more than forty years of K Set passenger service came to an end.

Most passengers onboard had no idea.

There was no public announcement. No farewell service. No opportunity for passengers, staff, enthusiasts or the wider community to recognise the contribution of one of Sydney's longest-serving train fleets.

For many people, the significance of the journey only became apparent afterwards.

More than just another train fleet

The K Sets were not simply another train fleet. Introduced in the early 1980s, they were among Sydney's first fully air-conditioned suburban trains and became a familiar part of everyday life for generations of commuters.

They carried students to school, workers to their jobs, families to major events and millions of passengers across the metropolitan network over more than four decades.

Their retirement marks the end of an era in Sydney's railway history.

A necessary retirement, but a missed farewell

No one is suggesting that the trains should have remained in service indefinitely. Fleet replacement is a normal and necessary part of operating a modern railway. Newer trains offer improved accessibility, reliability and passenger information systems.

However, the end of a fleet that served Sydney for more than forty years deserved acknowledgement.

Even a simple announcement would have allowed passengers to make an informed choice about whether they wished to experience a final journey. A short media statement, a commemorative trip, or even a social media post would have recognised the role these trains played in the lives of countless Sydneysiders.

Instead, the fleet quietly disappeared.

Why it matters

This is particularly disappointing because Sydney has a strong community of railway enthusiasts, preservation volunteers, transport historians and everyday passengers who value the city's transport heritage.

These groups routinely celebrate new infrastructure projects and fleet introductions. They should also be given the opportunity to recognise significant farewells.

The retirement of the K Sets was not merely an operational change. It was the conclusion of more than forty years of railway history.

That history deserved a proper farewell.

Instead, one of Sydney's most recognisable train fleets left the network without ceremony, without acknowledgement, and without the opportunity for the public to say goodbye.

After four decades of service, the K Sets went out not with a bang, but with barely a whisper.

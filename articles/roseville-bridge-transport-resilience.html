Media Release / 28 July 2026

Roseville Bridge incidents highlight the need for long-term transport resilience.

NSTA says this morning's disruptions demonstrate the vulnerability of the Lower North Shore and Northern Beaches transport network.

Media Release

28 July 2026

North Sydney Transport Association

The North Sydney Transport Association (NSTA) says this morning's traffic chaos around Roseville Bridge demonstrates the vulnerability of the Lower North Shore and Northern Beaches transport network, renewing calls for governments to plan higher-capacity public transport corridors alongside ongoing road improvements.

A truck rollover on Warringah Road shortly after 6:30am blocked two southbound lanes approaching Roseville Bridge, causing significant delays stretching back towards Frenchs Forest during the morning peak. A separate motorcycle collision northbound later added further disruption, leaving motorists and bus passengers caught in extensive congestion.

“Transport methods must be considered as a safer and more sustainable option as opposed to the serious risks associated with heavy-vehicle dependence on key corridors.”

NSTA Media and Communications Officer Sylvia Duong said the incidents demonstrated the need to reduce pressure on the Lower North Shore's existing road network and strengthen multimodal transport.

“To reduce pressure on the Lower North Shore's already congested road network, we need to strengthen multimodal integration with Transport for NSW.”

NSTA President Lachlan Steenson said the incidents were a reminder of how dependent the region remains on a small number of road corridors.

“Whenever a serious incident occurs around Roseville Bridge, the effects are felt across the entire Lower North Shore and Northern Beaches. Thousands of commuters, including bus passengers, are delayed because there are very few alternative transport options.”

Mr Steenson said that while the immediate priority was the safety of those involved, the disruption also raised broader questions about the resilience of the region's transport network.

“Today's priority is, of course, the safety of those involved and we thank emergency services for their response. However, once the immediate disruption has passed, we also need to ask what these events tell us about the resilience of our transport network.”

Planning beyond today's congestion

Earlier this year, the North Sydney Transport Association submitted its Future-Proofed Light Rail Network Strategy to the Northern Beaches Network Review.

The strategy proposes that Military Road, Spit Road and the wider Northern Beaches corridor be protected for future higher-capacity public transport.

Rather than replacing buses, the proposal recommends designing future bus-priority projects so they can eventually transition to a centre-running light rail corridor if demand continues to grow.

The proposal also recommends long-term planning for a replacement of the Spit Bridge that would accommodate dedicated public transport infrastructure, improved walking and cycling facilities, and provision for future light rail.

Mr Steenson said the Association was not suggesting that light rail would have prevented the day's incidents, but that the events reinforced the importance of creating a transport system with greater options, capacity and resilience.

“No transport system can eliminate crashes. What it can do is provide people with reliable alternatives when disruption occurs.”
“If one corridor is blocked, the impacts shouldn't ripple across an entire region. That's why we need to keep planning beyond today's congestion and ensure future infrastructure decisions don't close the door on higher-capacity public transport.”

A resilient network needs options

NSTA is encouraging Transport for NSW to continue improving bus services in the short term while ensuring road upgrades preserve opportunities for future rapid transit infrastructure across the Lower North Shore and Northern Beaches.

Young transport advocates respond as Sydney rail network descends into chaos

NSTA calls for urgent and clear communication with passengers as a major rail disruption affects Sydney during Friday evening peak.

14 August 2026 · North Sydney Transport Association

The North Sydney Transport Association (NSTA), a youth-led transport advocacy organisation, is calling for urgent and clear communication with passengers as a major rail disruption continues across Sydney during Friday evening peak hour.

Overhead wiring has come down in the vicinity of Redfern Station, with two of six tracks unavailable and three trains stranded in the affected corridor. Widespread delays and service changes are now affecting passengers across the network.

“We're young people who actually use this network. We catch these trains to school, to work, to see our friends and to get home. When the network falls apart like this, we feel it immediately.”

NSTA President Lachlan Steenson said the disruption was particularly concerning for young people who rely on public transport to get around Sydney.

“There are probably thousands of young people out there tonight who are trying to get home on their own and suddenly have no idea what their train is doing.”

Young people are passengers now

Mr Steenson said young passengers should be treated as an important part of the response to major disruptions.

“Young people aren't just passengers of the future. We're passengers right now, and we need to be part of the conversation about how this network works when things go wrong.”
“If you're 15 or 16 and you're stuck somewhere on the other side of Sydney, you can't necessarily just jump in a car and go home. Public transport might be your only realistic option.”

Focus on passengers first

The NSTA said the immediate priority should be the passengers currently affected by the incident.

“Right now, the most important thing is getting those people home. Three trains are stuck, services are being changed and there still isn't a clear timeframe for everything to return to normal.”

Mr Steenson said the organisation would not speculate about the cause of the incident while the response was ongoing.

“We're not going to sit here and guess why the wires came down. That's for the people investigating it. Our job is to speak up for the passengers dealing with the consequences.”

Advice for passengers

The NSTA urged passengers to check official updates before travelling and to allow significantly more time for journeys tonight.

Regan Hopes for Beaches Link & Trackless Trams as Beaches' Future Transport Developments

Following a meeting with Wakehurst MP Michael Regan, NSTA examines his views on Beaches Link, bus network reform and the possibility of trackless trams on the Northern Beaches.

Lachlan Steenson • 21 April 2026

Type
Interview

Date
21 April 2026

Topic
Northern Beaches transport

Back to news

On the 8th of April, myself and Shawn Huang were fortunate enough to be welcomed into the office of Michael Regan, Member for Wakehurst in the NSW Legislative Assembly, to speak with him regarding public transport in the region, the Beaches Link motorway and what he sees in the area’s transit future.

The meeting came after the news of Michael Regan’s advocacy for the unshelving of the Beaches Link motorway, whom the Sydney Morning Herald quotes as saying “The [Beaches Link] project sits on the shelf ready to be done and delivered. We’ll continue to push for it because it’s a vital link … for the upper northern beaches”.

Now, with the world in one of the biggest fuel crises in decades, and with many of Australia’s cities engaging in transport expansion projects, the question we were asking ourselves going into the meeting was “Why build it now?”.

Regan’s response to this question, when we verbalised it, was focused less on the short-term fuel situation and more on having long-term infrastructure ready. Projects like Beaches Link are planned across years and decades, not in response to immediate conditions like fuel supply, and having a shelf-ready project like the Beaches Link able to be built does enable the region to be in a favourable position when funding and political priorities do switch.

The recent prevalence of electric vehicles also acts as a key reason behind the tunnel’s ability to stay viable even during the current fuel shortage.

Improvements to the bus network can be expected, however, with Regan not only promoting the opening of consultations for the Northern Beaches Network Review, but also noting that with the advent of the Keoride “On Demand” system, there comes the opportunity of a new way to apply bus transportation effectively in the region.

Indeed, travelling to the office from each of our homes in the North Shore and the Hills required taking the 160X from Chatswood to Dee Why, a route which Regan expects to become “Route B2” in the form of an expansion of the B-Line, which currently runs from Wynyard to Dee Why.

Overall, this reflects to a reasonable degree of accuracy the shift that is currently occurring on the beaches, in terms of strengthening high frequency bus corridors in the area. The B-Line already exhibits strong demand, as Regan referenced in our meeting, and the expansion of other high frequency services could see a relatively fast uplift in usage. The B-Line, as Regan noted, also has the characteristic of running empty against the peak flow, with a number travelling back up from Wynyard to Mona Vale empty whilst the morning peak occurs.

A recurring theme throughout the discussion was Regan’s enthusiasm for a trackless tram trial and rollout in the region, which Regan sees as a potential middle-ground solution for the Northern Beaches. These vehicles, which operate on rubber tyres but aim to replicate the ride and capacity of light rail, were described by Regan as just like trams. While the terminology may be debated, the broader idea reflects a desire for higher-capacity, higher-quality transit without the cost of full rail construction.

Regan’s support for this mode is closely tied to his broader argument in favour of the Beaches Link tunnel. He emphasised that, unlike a dedicated metro tunnel, a motorway tunnel could support multiple transport modes simultaneously, including general traffic, buses, trucks, and potentially trackless trams. In his view, this allows for greater overall utility from a single major infrastructure investment. Trackless trams, he argued, could make use of such infrastructure easily while also continuing beyond the tunnel onto existing road corridors, like Pittwater Road, while offering a level of flexibility that fixed rail cannot.

At the same time, he acknowledged the strengths of metro systems, noting that there are scenarios where they are able to move more people. However, he argued that the single-purpose nature of their tunnels, moving only metro trains, contrasts with the adaptability of road-based infrastructure. Cost was another significant factor raised, with Regan suggesting that a metro project to the Northern Beaches could cost the same, if not be more expensive, in comparison to Beaches Link, while only serving a single mode. With government funding currently focused on Sydney’s southwest and western growth areas, he indicated that such an investment may also be unlikely in the near future.

Regan also pointed to travel patterns within the Northern Beaches themselves, noting that most Northern Beaches residents worked within the region. It became clear, as such, that local connectivity has a much more important role in the Northern Beaches in comparison to other regions of Sydney. Transport developments, aside from the Beaches Link, are in fact targeting this, with the previously mentioned Network Review for the Northern Beaches’ buses underway, set to enhance this connectivity. In this context, reliable intra-regional services are in fact very crucial, as opposed to services leaving the region.

Finally, the conversation turned to feasibility. When asked whether he would support a metro to the Northern Beaches if it were proven to be more feasible than Beaches Link, Regan responded that he would, though he suggested such an outcome was unlikely.

Overall, the discussion highlighted the key features of future transport developments in the region, prioritising flexibility, cost-effectiveness, and intra-regional connectivity, even if that means pursuing less conventional solutions. The idea of trackless trams as a feasible mode in the Beaches represents an emerging part of that conversation, and one that may continue to shape the future of transport planning on the Northern Beaches.

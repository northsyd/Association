Proposal

Crows Nest, Burlington Street should become Crows Nest Village Interchange

A clearer name for Burlington Street’s bus interchange would improve wayfinding, distinguish it from Crows Nest Metro Station, and better reflect its role as a local transfer point.

Published April 2026 • North Sydney Transport Association

Type
Proposal article

Date
April 2026

Topic
Bus stops, wayfinding, Crows Nest

Back to news

Summary

The North Sydney Transport Association supports renaming the Burlington Street bus stops at Crows Nest to Crows Nest Village Interchange. The current name, “Crows Nest, Burlington Street”, does not fully reflect the role of the stops as a multi-route transfer point within the Crows Nest village centre.

The proposal also recommends standardising the stand letters to A, B, C and D, replacing the current mismatch between physical signage and digital journey planning systems.

The problem with the current name

Nearby stops use names such as “Crows Nest Station, Pacific Highway”, while the Burlington Street interchange is labelled “Crows Nest, Burlington Street”. This creates a risk that passengers assume the different stops are part of one single interchange, even though they serve different functions and are not directly co-located.

The Burlington Street stops primarily serve the local bus network and sit inside the pedestrian and retail centre of Crows Nest. The name should reflect this local role rather than being blurred with the nearby metro station.

“The proposed name clarifies structure, improves usability, and better reflects the role of Burlington Street as a local interchange.”

Why “Village Interchange” works

Crows Nest is widely understood as a village centre on the Lower North Shore. The term “Village” reflects the area’s walkable, human-scale character and distinguishes Burlington Street from the larger-scale metro infrastructure nearby.

The word “Interchange” is also important. Burlington Street functions as a multi-route transfer point, not just a set of isolated bus stops. Naming it as an interchange would make that role clearer to passengers, especially visitors, elderly passengers and infrequent users.

Government responses so far

The proposal was first sent through the Geographical Names Board process, before being directed to Transport for NSW. Transport for NSW initially referred the matter toward council-owned infrastructure, while North Sydney Council clarified that TfNSW remains responsible for bus stop signs.

In a later response, Transport for NSW confirmed that the proposal had been reviewed for the Burlington Street stop IDs and stated that “Crows Nest Shops” would be supported under current naming conventions. TfNSW also advised that the broader “Crows Nest Village, Burlington Street” proposal would be referred for consideration.

NSTA’s position

NSTA believes that “Crows Nest Village Interchange” is clearer, more useful and more accurate than the existing name. It better describes the location, the function of the stops, and their relationship to the surrounding village centre.

As Crows Nest continues to change following the opening of the metro station, clear naming will become more important. Renaming Burlington Street now would avoid future confusion and improve the legibility of the local transport network.

Full submission

The following is the original proposal content, reformatted for publication on the NSTA website.

1. Mismatched stand numbering

  • Current stand identification is inconsistent across platforms.
  • Physical signage uses stands 1, 3, 4 and 5.
  • Digital systems, including apps and GTFS data, use A, C, D and E.
  • Stand “B” is missing entirely in both systems.
  • There is no logical correspondence between the physical and digital naming.

This creates confusion for passengers navigating between signage and apps, especially new or infrequent users. It increases the likelihood of missed services or boarding at incorrect locations.

The proposed correction is to standardise the stands as A, B, C and D, creating a consistent cross-platform wayfinding system.

2. Village identity

Crows Nest is widely recognised as a village centre within the Lower North Shore. The Burlington Street interchange is located within the retail and pedestrian core, rather than on a major arterial road.

“Village” is already used in local business identity, community language and planning context. The name reflects the area’s human-scale, walkable environment and strong local character.

3. Distinguishing from Sydney Metro bus stops

Nearby bus stops are named “Crows Nest Station, Pacific Highway”, while the Burlington Street stops are named “Crows Nest, Burlington Street”. Both share the phrase “Crows Nest”, but they are not co-located and serve different functions.

The current naming creates a false assumption of direct interchange. The metro station does not recognise Burlington Street as its primary interchange, so Burlington Street should not be implicitly tied to the station through naming.

The addition of “Village” clearly separates the two nodes.

4. Mode differentiation

The current naming barely distinguishes between the local bus interchange and the metro station. The proposed name introduces a clear modal distinction and supports intuitive understanding of bus-to-bus transfers versus metro access.

This aligns with the principle that names should reflect function and mode.

5. Wayfinding clarity

The current naming creates false proximity assumptions. Passengers may alight at incorrect stops or miss intended connections, particularly visitors, infrequent users and elderly passengers.

Renaming would reduce navigation errors and cognitive load, improving overall network legibility.

6. Legibility in digital systems

Journey planners rely on clear and distinct stop names. Similar names reduce search accuracy and readability in trip planning.

“Crows Nest Village, Burlington Street” provides a more unique identifier and improves clarity in apps and maps.

7. Precedent in naming practice

Sydney already uses distinct names for nearby but separate nodes. For example, Town Hall railway station and Gadigal railway station are close to one another but have different names reflecting their different roles.

Applying similar logic at Crows Nest would ensure consistency with established practice.

8. Historical pre-eminence

The Crows Nest bus interchange predates the metro station and has long served as the primary bus node in the area.

The current naming diminishes its prominence, while renaming would restore recognition of its established role.

9. Interchange function recognition

Burlington Street functions as a multi-route transfer point. The current naming treats the stands as isolated stops.

“Interchange” accurately reflects the role of the location, signals transfer opportunities and aligns with naming practices for other key transport nodes.

10. Consistency with urban structure

Burlington Street is a local street within a village centre, while Pacific Highway is a major arterial corridor.

Naming should reflect this local-versus-regional function. “Village Interchange” reinforces the distinction.

11. Avoiding redundant geographic labels

Repeated use of “Crows Nest” as a qualifier without additional descriptors reduces clarity. Adding “Village” introduces spatial hierarchy while retaining the geographic reference.

12. Passenger mental mapping

Effective naming aligns with how users think:

  • “Station” suggests a rail or metro hub.
  • “Village” suggests a local centre.
  • “Interchange” suggests a transfer point.

The proposed name clearly communicates function, location type and network role.

13. Future-proofing

Metro expansion will increase passenger volumes and reliance on clear naming. Current ambiguity is likely to worsen over time.

Renaming now avoids future reactive changes.

14. Signage efficiency

The current naming requires additional descriptors such as street names and stand letters. A strong interchange name reduces reliance on secondary information and enables cleaner stop signage, onboard announcements and maps.

15. Local landmark recognition

The interchange features distinctive heritage-listed shelters and has a long-standing transport role. It functions as a recognisable local landmark, and its name should reflect that status.

16. Core argument summary

The current naming implies a single unified “Crows Nest” transport node. In reality, multiple distinct facilities exist, each with different roles and locations.

The proposed name clarifies structure, improves usability and aligns with best practice.

Email history following submissions

1. Geographical Names Board of NSW

Received: 7 April 2026

“We commend the group for its strong interest in public transport and for the clear, well-reasoned and well-researched arguments you have outlined in support of the proposed name ‘Crows Nest Village Interchange’.”

The Geographical Names Board advised that Transport for NSW is the state asset holder responsible for proposing names for public transport facilities in NSW, including bus interchanges and associated infrastructure.

The Board recommended that the proposal and supporting rationale be directed to Transport for NSW. If TfNSW chose to progress a naming proposal, it would then be formally referred back to the Geographical Names Board for assessment.

2. Transport for NSW response A

Received: 14 April 2026

Transport for NSW thanked the group for the detailed proposal and acknowledged the analysis provided regarding wayfinding, customer clarity and the local transport context following the opening of Crows Nest Metro Station.

TfNSW initially advised that the bus stop shelter and seating at Stop ID 206016, Miller Street at Falcon Street, were local council assets rather than TfNSW assets. It recommended forwarding the case to council for consideration.

3. North Sydney Council response

Received: 1 May 2026

North Sydney Council confirmed that the naming of bus stops is a matter for Transport for NSW and that the initial enquiry had been correctly addressed to TfNSW.

Council stated that bus stop signs are the property of TfNSW and that Council is unable to alter these signs. Council also clarified that while some bus shelters may have separate names linked to heritage, the proposal related to bus stop naming rather than shelter naming.

4. Transport for NSW response B

Received: 11 May 2026

Transport for NSW thanked the group for resubmitting the proposal and clarified that the request related specifically to stop naming conventions, rather than infrastructure or service changes.

TfNSW reviewed the submission regarding the stops located along Burlington Street, Crows Nest, specifically Stop IDs 206540, 206541, 206544 and 206546, currently collectively described as “Crows Nest, Burlington Street”.

TfNSW advised that a stop name change to “Crows Nest Shops” would be supported under current customer information and naming guidelines, as it aligns with established conventions referencing recognised activity centres.

However, TfNSW also stated that the proposed name of “Crows Nest Village, Burlington Street” introduced a broader place-based descriptor not currently standard across the network. The proposal and supporting rationale were formally referred to the Transport for NSW team for consideration.

Conclusion

The Crows Nest Village Interchange proposal remains an example of how detailed local transport advocacy can identify practical passenger-facing improvements. Even where naming conventions are complex, clearer public transport language can improve wayfinding, reduce confusion and better reflect how places function.

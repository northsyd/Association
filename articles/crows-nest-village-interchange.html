Crows Nest, Burlington Street should become Crows Nest Village Interchange

A clearer name for Burlington Street’s bus interchange would improve wayfinding, distinguish it from Crows Nest Metro Station, and better reflect its role as a local transfer point.

Published April 2026 • North Sydney Transport Association

Type
Proposal article

Date
April 2026

Topic
Bus stops, wayfinding, Crows Nest

Back to news

Summary

The North Sydney Transport Association supports renaming the Burlington Street bus stops at Crows Nest to Crows Nest Village Interchange. The current name, “Crows Nest, Burlington Street”, does not fully reflect the role of the stops as a multi-route transfer point within the Crows Nest village centre.

The proposal also recommends standardising the stand letters to A, B, C and D, replacing the current mismatch between physical signage and digital journey planning systems.

The problem with the current name

At present, the Burlington Street stops are named separately from the nearby metro station stops, but the distinction is not especially clear. Nearby stops use names such as “Crows Nest Station, Pacific Highway”, while the Burlington Street interchange is labelled “Crows Nest, Burlington Street”.

This creates a risk that passengers assume the different stops are part of one single interchange, even though they serve different functions and are not directly co-located. The Burlington Street stops primarily serve the local bus network and sit inside the pedestrian and retail centre of Crows Nest.

“The proposed name clarifies structure, improves usability, and better reflects the role of Burlington Street as a local interchange.”

Why “Village Interchange” works

Crows Nest is widely understood as a village centre on the Lower North Shore. The term “Village” reflects the area’s walkable, human-scale character and distinguishes Burlington Street from the larger-scale metro infrastructure nearby.

The word “Interchange” is also important. Burlington Street functions as a multi-route transfer point, not just a set of isolated bus stops. Naming it as an interchange would make that role clearer to passengers, especially visitors, elderly passengers and infrequent users.

Stand numbering should also be fixed

The proposal identifies a mismatch between physical signage and digital systems. Physical signs use stand numbers such as 1, 3, 4 and 5, while digital systems use stand letters such as A, C, D and E. Stand B is missing entirely.

NSTA recommends standardising the stands as A, B, C and D. This would make apps, signage and passenger directions easier to understand.

Government responses so far

The Geographical Names Board advised that Transport for NSW is the responsible authority for initiating public transport infrastructure naming proposals. Transport for NSW initially referred the matter toward council-owned infrastructure, while North Sydney Council clarified that TfNSW remains responsible for bus stop signs.

In a later response, Transport for NSW confirmed that the proposal had been reviewed for the Burlington Street stop IDs and stated that “Crows Nest Shops” would be supported under current naming conventions. TfNSW also advised that the broader “Crows Nest Village, Burlington Street” proposal would be referred for consideration.

NSTA’s position

NSTA believes that “Crows Nest Village Interchange” is clearer, more useful and more accurate than the existing name. It better describes the location, the function of the stops, and their relationship to the surrounding village centre.

As Crows Nest continues to change following the opening of the metro station, clear naming will become more important. Renaming Burlington Street now would avoid future confusion and improve the legibility of the local transport network.

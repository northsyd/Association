NSTA Calls for Pacific National Rail Safety Review Following Hunter Runaway Train Incident

The North Sydney Transport Association is calling on Pacific National to review and strengthen rail safety procedures following an incident involving an unattended freight train in the Hunter Valley.

The North Sydney Transport Association (NSTA) is calling on Pacific National to review and strengthen its rail safety procedures following an incident involving an unattended freight train in the Hunter region.

The incident, which occurred on the morning of 25 July 2026, has raised questions among transport advocates about the safeguards in place to prevent uncontrolled train movements and ensure the safety of rail workers, passengers and the wider community.

The unmanned runaway coal train travelled several kilometres through the Hunter Valley in New South Wales, beginning movement at Camberwell before coming to a stop near Glennies Creek.

Strengthening operational safety procedures

The NSTA is calling for Pacific National to introduce additional operational requirements, including ensuring that train crew members do not exit locomotives or trains in operational environments without another authorised person present and on board where practicable.

While incidents of this nature are uncommon, the NSTA believes that every opportunity to improve safety procedures across the rail industry should be taken seriously.

“Rail safety relies on layers of protection. While incidents like this are obviously quite uncommon in this day and age, every opportunity to improve safety procedures, especially on non-passenger networks, should be taken seriously.”

— Lachlan Steenson, President of the North Sydney Transport Association

The NSTA believes that stronger procedures, clearer communication protocols and improved risk management practices can help prevent similar incidents from occurring in the future.

“Operators shouldn’t have to rely on luck when it comes to the safe operation of heavy rail. Ensuring workers, in such a freight-heavy region as the Hunter, have appropriate support and backup when performing safety-critical duties should be a priority across the industry.”

— Sylvia Duong, Head of the NSTA Commission into the Newcastle Region

Industry transparency and lessons learned

The NSTA acknowledges the professionalism of emergency services, rail workers and authorities involved in responding to the incident.

The Association is also calling for greater transparency from freight operators regarding safety reviews following significant operational incidents, ensuring lessons can be shared across the broader rail industry.

As rail networks continue to play a critical role in moving freight across New South Wales, the NSTA believes maintaining strong safety cultures and continually reviewing operational practices are essential.

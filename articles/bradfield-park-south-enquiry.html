Public Space or TfNSW Works Depot? Youth Advocates Launch Inquiry into Bradfield Park South

NSTA establishes an independent commission examining the transport, accessibility and community impacts surrounding the proposed use of Bradfield Park South.

Media Release

27 July 2026

Organisation
North Sydney Transport Association

Commission lead
Charlie Green

The North Sydney Transport Association (NSTA) has announced the establishment of an independent commission examining the transport, accessibility and community impacts surrounding the proposed use of Bradfield Park South.

Part of Bradfield Park South is currently fenced off for use as a works depot by Transport for NSW (TfNSW). The commission will examine the implications of the site's use and consider the concerns of residents, community members and other stakeholders.

Hearing directly from the community

The commission will be led by NSTA Vice President Charlie Green, who will attend an upcoming Bradfield Park South community meeting as part of the organisation's evidence-gathering process.

The community meeting is being independently organised by local stakeholders and is not affiliated with NSTA. The Association's attendance forms part of its investigation and consultation process.

What the commission will examine

The commission will examine several aspects of the proposed use of Bradfield Park South, including:

  • The transport objectives associated with the proposed use of the site;
  • Community feedback and concerns regarding the proposal;
  • Accessibility impacts for pedestrians, people with disability and other park users;
  • Consultation processes surrounding the proposal; and
  • Potential opportunities for improved outcomes.

Balancing infrastructure and community needs

NSTA says the inquiry will consider how major transport infrastructure and construction activities interact with public space and community needs.

“People deserve clarity about why public space is being fenced off and how long it will remain inaccessible. Our commission will bring transparency to Transport for NSW’s decisions and ensure community concerns are taken seriously. We're making sure that the community's voice is not an afterthought in major transport decisions. It's more than a construction site, it encapsulates a history and a place people seek for recreation.”

Sylvia Duong, Communications Officer at NSTA

“Transport has been building and operating the Sydney Harbour Tunnel from this park for over 30 years. It’s time for them to be held accountable, and return this land to the community in the state that it was taken from them.”

Lachlan Steenson, President of NSTA

The role of young people

The Association says the commission will also consider the role young people can play in discussions about transport, infrastructure and public space.

NSTA believes young people should have opportunities to contribute to decisions that shape the places and transport networks they use today.

Community consultation

The upcoming community meeting will provide an opportunity for the commission to hear directly from residents and other stakeholders about their experiences and concerns.

NSTA says evidence gathered through the meeting and other consultation will contribute to the commission's broader investigation.

NSTA submission raised during council debate

The North Sydney Transport Association was referenced during debate at a North Sydney Council meeting considering Item 9.5, the Council’s submission to Transport for NSW regarding the Northern Beaches Network Review.

During the debate, Councillor Welch stated that amendments proposed by Councillor Santer appeared to arise from a submission circulated to councillors by the North Sydney Transport Association.

“I think they arise from a submission circulated to all councillors from the North Sydney Transport Association.”

For NSTA, this represented a significant milestone. The Association’s work was not only submitted to councillors, but directly entered the council debate itself.

Amendments proposed

Councillor Santer proposed two additions to the draft submission.

The first sought stronger wording around active transport:

“Deliver strategic cycleway corridors, including protected connections along Military Road and Pacific Highway.”

The second proposed the investigation of a stronger public transport connection between the Lower North Shore and the Northern Beaches:

“Explore the establishment of a dedicated public transport link from the Lower North Shore to the Northern Beaches.”

These additions aligned with several themes of NSTA advocacy, including stronger active transport corridors, better cross-harbour and cross-regional public transport links, and long-term strategic planning for the Lower North Shore.

Why this matters

NSTA was founded on the idea that students and young people can contribute meaningfully to transport planning debates.

The council debate showed that well-researched local submissions can influence public discussion. Even when an association is small, clear arguments can still enter formal decision-making spaces.

The reference also helped demonstrate the value of preparing submissions that are specific, practical and connected to current consultation processes.

Outcome

Councillor Welch accepted the additions proposed by Councillor Santer, and Councillor Santer confirmed he would second them.

While the discussion remained focused on Council’s submission to Transport for NSW, the moment marked an important example of NSTA’s advocacy being recognised in a formal council setting.

Looking ahead

NSTA will continue engaging with transport consultations, local government processes and public planning debates across North Sydney and Sydney more broadly.

The Association welcomes constructive discussion on public transport, active transport, corridor planning and long-term infrastructure needs.

Primary source: Council transcript

The following transcript records the section of debate in which the North Sydney Transport Association was referenced.

Mayor:
Item 9.5 — Northern Beaches Network Review submission to Transport for NSW. Is there a mover?

Councillor Welch:
I move the recommendation.

Mayor:
Is there a seconder?

Councillor Santer:
Seconded.

Mayor:
Councillor Santer, do you wish to speak to the item?

Councillor Welch:
Thank you. If Councillor Santer has distributed some additions he would like made to the recommendation, I think they arise from a submission circulated to all councillors from the North Sydney Transport Association. So if you would put your additions, Councillor Santer.

Councillor Santer:
Thank you. I support the motion as well, but would like to suggest some minor amendments to the draft submission.

Firstly, that the wording include:

“Deliver strategic cycleway corridors, including protected connections along Military Road and Pacific Highway.”

The text already refers to Pacific Highway, but it is not specifically included in the draft.

Secondly, a new point:

“Explore the establishment of a dedicated public transport link from the Lower North Shore to the Northern Beaches.”

Mayor:
Councillor Welch, will you accept those additions?

Councillor Welch:
Thank you.

Mayor:
And Councillor Santer, you will second?

Councillor Santer:
Yes.

Councillor Welch:
Thank you, Madam Mayor. This is an excellent submission and I congratulate staff.

Original submission

Submission to North Sydney Council
Regarding the Northern Beaches Bus Network Review
From the North Sydney Transport Association

The North Sydney Transport Association urges North Sydney Council to advocate, in its submission to Transport for NSW regarding the Northern Beaches Network Review, for any future upgrades to Military Road to incorporate provisions for potential future mass transit conversion, particularly light rail infrastructure.

Military Road is one of Sydney’s most significant public transport corridors, carrying extremely high bus volumes between the Northern Beaches, Lower North Shore, and the Sydney CBD. While the current review is focused on road vehicle operations, the corridor’s long-term strategic importance means that infrastructure decisions made today should avoid limiting future transport capacity improvements.

The Association specifically requests that Council recommend:

The preservation or construction of specialised medians along Military Road, designed in a way that could accommodate future light rail infrastructure without requiring major reconstruction.

A method by which this could be done is by shifting bus operations into the newly constructed median alignment, turning bus lanes to car space lost by the expanded median, avoiding the need for excessive widening.

The implementation of bus priority infrastructure in said median is designed to allow future conversion or adaptation to higher-capacity transit modes.

This would be done in a similar manner to the design approach used for the Sydney CBD and South East Light Rail corridor along Anzac Parade and Alison Road, where corridor space has been structured to support both current bus efficiency and long-term rapid transit opportunities. We also recommend streetscape and road renewal works that avoid permanently constraining the corridor through excessive kerbside widening, fragmented lane arrangements, or other designs incompatible with future fixed-track transit.

Military Road’s constrained geography and growing transport demand mean that long-term corridor preservation is essential. Even if light rail delivery remains a future consideration rather than a current commitment, ensuring that corridor upgrades remain compatible with future higher-capacity transit represents prudent strategic planning.

The North Sydney Transport Association believes the Northern Beaches corridor requires long-term transport vision beyond incremental bus improvements alone, and that Council should encourage TfNSW to future-proof the corridor wherever practical.
